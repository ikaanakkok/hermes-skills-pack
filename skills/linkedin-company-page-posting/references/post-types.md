# Post types — LinkedIn company page

Pick the type from what the human sends (text, photos, a date, a job description).
Each entry: when to use it, the shape, opener patterns, and what goes wrong.

## 1. Announcement

Use for: a new program, partnership, milestone, policy change, anything with news value.
Photos: a poster, a group shot, or the thing itself.

```
<hook: what is happening, one concrete sentence>

<what it means / who it affects, 1-3 lines>

<the one detail people need: date, eligibility, where>

<single CTA>

<#org> <#topic>
```

Openers: "We're opening X to anyone who...", "Starting next month, X."
Avoid: "We are thrilled to announce" — say the thing instead.

## 2. Event

Use for: sessions, workshops, panels, competitions — before (invitation) or after (recap;
see type 4).
Photos: poster, prep shots, previous edition.

```
<hook: what, when — put the date in the hook, not the last line>

<who is speaking / what will be covered: name, role, organization, why it is interesting>

<2-3 bullets on what people will actually get>

<where, what time, who can come, how to sign up>

<#org> <#topic>
```

Rules: date and location appear twice (hook + detail block). If capacity is limited, say
so. Registration link goes in the first comment unless the deadline is imminent.

## 3. Recruitment

Use for: open roles, member intake, volunteer calls, applications.
Photos: team shots, workspace, the work itself — never a stock handshake.

```
<hook: what you are looking for, in one line>

<who should apply — be explicit about level, time commitment, location>

<what the work actually is, 2-3 concrete bullets>

<how to apply + deadline>
```

Rules: deadline in the body, never only in the link. No "rockstar", no "family", no
unpaid-work euphemisms. Say the commitment in hours. One application channel only.

## 4. Recap

Use for: after an event, end of a program, a project shipped.

```
<one line: what happened, with a real number>

<thank-you / what stuck with people, 1-2 sentences>

<what the photos show>

<one question to the audience>
```

Rules: tag the guest(s) and partners actually involved. Name the team that did the work.
Numbers must be real — an honest "12 people, small room, good conversation" beats an
inflated claim.

## 5. Work-in-progress share

Use for: process posts — research, a draft, a prototype, a rehearsal.

```
<what you are working on, one sentence>

<how it is going: what you solved, what you have not (honesty is the interesting part)>

<who is on it / how many>

<next step>
```

## 6. Team / member introduction

Short: name, role or year, what they care about, what they do on the page. 2-3 lines per
person, match names to photos if several. Get consent before publishing anyone's photo
or title.

## Tagging

- Tag the guest and, where appropriate, their organization — they often reshare.
- Get the human's confirmation for every tag; a wrong tag is worse than no tag.
- Tag a parent organization or partner account only when the content genuinely concerns
  them.
- Never tag someone to borrow their audience.
