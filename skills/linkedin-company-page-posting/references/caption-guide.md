# Caption guide - LinkedIn company page

## Voice

The page speaks as the organization, in first person plural ("we"); the reader is a
member, participant, peer or candidate — not a customer. Warm, direct, specific.

- Say what actually happened: real names, real numbers (attendance, speaker's role and
  company, number of sessions). Specificity is what makes a page credible.
- One piece of personality per post. A page is people, not a brand deck.
- Second person for invitations: "anyone who wants to join", "we'd love to see you
  there".
- If the organization is small, informal or volunteer-run, sound like that. Corporate
  register on a small page reads as fake.

## Not corporate

| Corporate (no) | Human (yes) |
|---|---|
| "was successfully carried out" | "we ran", "we hosted" |
| "creating value for our stakeholders" | "we learned this together" |
| "contact us for further information" | "DM us, ask anything" |
| "our industry-leading speaker" | "our guest X, who does Y" |
| signature block / formal sign-off | none |

Never a slogan, never "our mission", never a signature block.

## Structure

1. **Hook (line 1, <= 140 chars)** — what happened or what is coming. Concrete.
2. **What it was** — 1-3 lines of context (who came, what you did, how it went).
3. **Detail or thanks** — tag the speaker/partner, name the team, cite the number.
4. **CTA or invitation** — one question for a recap, one date + one action for an
   announcement.
5. **Hashtags** — max 3: one organization tag, one topical.

Blank line between blocks. LinkedIn shows ~3 lines before "...see more", so the hook
must work alone.

## Links: body vs first comment

- External links in the body reduce reach on LinkedIn; the post itself is not penalised
  but the click leaves the platform.
- Default: write the post without a link, then add the link as the **first comment**
  immediately after publishing.
- If you promise "link in the comments", post that comment in the same session — a
  promise with no comment is a broken post.
- Put the link in the body when the link *is* the point (registration deadline today,
  application form), and accept the reach cost.

## Language

One language per post. If the organization posts in a language other than English, keep
the same structure and idiom in that language — do not translate an English draft
word-for-word, and never mix two languages in one post.

## Anti-patterns

- "Hey LinkedIn family!" / "Big news!"
- 10+ hashtags, hashtag walls
- "industry leader" claims with nothing behind them
- Tagging people or companies who were not involved
- Inventing attendance numbers, guest names, quotes or dates
- "details in the comments" with no comment ready
- Emoji as bullet points in every line

## Before sending the draft to the human

- Count characters (<= 3000).
- Every name, number and date traces to something the human said or supplied.
- Read the first 140 characters — if they waste the slot, rewrite.
- Would a real person at this organization post this? If it reads like a press release,
  rewrite.
